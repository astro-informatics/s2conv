Top, from left to right: (a) the temperature anisotropy, shown as colour in μK on a diverging scale, red hotter and blue colder than the mean, with the polarisation, smoothed by the spin-2 convolution $\mathcal{C}_{2\to2}$ with a Gaussian of width $4/L$ radians so that the sticks follow its large-scale pattern, drawn as headless sticks along the direction of polarisation, at the angle $\arg(Q+\mathrm{i}U)/2$, whose lengths grow in proportion to the polarised intensity from 35 per cent of the largest length; (b) the polarised intensity $|Q+\mathrm{i}U|$, shown as colour in μK on a sequential scale, with the same sticks; (c) the $E$ mode, shown as colour in μK on a diverging scale. Both modes show residual Galactic emission along the equator of the maps, and the $B$ mode, which on these scales is dominated by noise and by these residuals, has an rms of 79 per cent of that of the $E$ mode.  The decomposition and its inverse are single multipliers in harmonic space, $-\tfrac12$ and $\pm\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}2$ one way and $-1$ and $\mp\mathrm{i}$ the other, so that the polarisation synthesised from the two modes reproduces the measured polarisation to the precision of the arithmetic.  Starting from the map of the polarisation, computing the maps of the $E$ and $B$ modes and synthesising the map of the polarisation again, including all the harmonic transforms, recovers the map to a relative $L^2$ error of 1.3e-13, set by the rounding errors of the transforms.
